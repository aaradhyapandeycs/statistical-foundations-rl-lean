## Independent reconstruction and verdict

The lower-bound statement quantifies over every Q estimator and then asserts the existence of an MDP with specified S and A on which the error probability is at least 1/4. It permits S=1, A=16. The source explicitly gives the learner the deterministic reward table in advance. With one state, every transition returns that state, so the known-input estimator `r(s,a) + γ max_b r(s,b)/(1−γ)` does not consult observations or unknown transitions and equals the optimal Q pointwise. Consequently its strict-error event is empty for every nonnegative ε and has mass zero under every data law. The separately reviewed `docs/critic-one-state-parameters.md` establishes that the numerical assumptions admit S=1, A=16, γ=2/3 and N=1 for every positive proposed universal constant, with an appropriate positive ε.

The staged Lean file correctly proves that conclusion for the project's actual infinite forced-action trajectory Q of the constructed optimal greedy policy. `oneStateKnownRewardEstimator` is a single definition taking the known reward and discount as inputs and ignoring every data value; its body does not mention an MDP or transition. For every reward and discount, every subsingleton-state MDP with those inputs, and every data value, `oneStateKnownRewardEstimator_exact` equates it to that MDP's actual greedy trajectory Q. The reward and discount equalities identify known inputs and are not restrictions on the unknown transition. Its norm-error theorem is pointwise and the final theorem takes an arbitrary measure on an arbitrary measurable data type, not an assumed iid law; the event is proved literally empty. Its ε≥0 hypothesis covers the source's strictly positive range, and no sample-size or success-probability assumption is hidden. The finite pi norm is the sup norm over all state-action coordinates. The estimator is constant in data; a separate theorem packaging its joint measurability in the known inputs is not present here.

`oneState_trajectoryQ_le` establishes optimality against every stationary randomized policy, and `oneState_rewardGreedy_trajectoryQ` identifies the actual Q of a reward-maximizing policy. These declarations use the existing trajectory-to-Bellman and optimality bridges, rather than redefining Q as the fixed point. Their quantified policy scope is stationary; they do not independently establish supremacy over arbitrary history-dependent policies. The source-shaped estimator obstruction, numerical feasibility, and any statistical experiment are separate declarations rather than one formal negation of the complete source theorem; this review does not claim such a combined export or certify a policy-estimator lower-bound refutation.
