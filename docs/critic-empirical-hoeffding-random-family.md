## Independent reconstruction

Given N>0 iid samples from a fixed finite state-action transition row, their empirical row is the vector of counts divided by N. For 0<δ<1 and a random finite set 𝒱 of nonnegative vectors independent of that empirical row, the source asserts a strict probability >1−δ that the source logarithmic square-root bound holds simultaneously for all V∈𝒱. The random set can have variable cardinality. When 𝒱 is empty the event is vacuous and has probability 1; when V has zero norm its deviation and threshold are both zero. Independence from the empirical row is sufficient; demanding independence from the full ordered sample would be stronger than the source.

## Findings

1. `empiricalRowLaw` is the pushforward of the previously constructed iid product sample law through the actual empirical frequency function. `empiricalRows` contains every possible such row, finite because N and State are finite. The family bad event tests the actual difference of row expectations against `familyRadius`; the imported `familyRadius_eq_source` identifies that radius with the source expression.
2. `empiricalRowLaw_familyBad_lt` supplies strict <δ failure for each deterministic nonnegative finite family; it uses the independently proved finite-family strict result and explicitly branches on empty family. The zero-norm case is covered in the imported family result. No lower bound on individual row probabilities is used.
3. `product_supportedFamilyBad_lt` works on an arbitrary probability law of family codes and the actual empirical-row law. It permits variable, empty and continuously varying finite families, assumes nonnegativity only almost everywhere, and proves strict <δ after integration. Its section equality relies on the fact that the empirical row is supported on the finite attainable set. Strictness is not silently weakened: the proof uses pointwise strict family bounds and strict monotonicity of the nonnegative integral for a nonzero probability measure.
4. `randomFamily_empiricalRow_success_gt` assumes the actual ordered draws have the iid product law, but the independence premise is between the family **code and empirical row**, not the ordered draw. It establishes the joint product law by `IndepFun` and maps through the actual finite-row pushforward. The resulting event and strict >1−δ conclusion have the source cardinality-dependent threshold. The auxiliary `hVmeas` and `hbad` premises state measurability of the family nonnegativity event and of its failure sections; they do not assume the desired numerical probability bound.
5. `randomFiniteFamily_empiricalRow_success_gt_vectorSigma` specializes to a direct random Finset of real vectors. Its row independence uses `rowVectorSpace`, the σ-algebra induced by the ordinary real-coordinate vector. The finite-range lemma proves the same independence for the discrete row σ-algebra because every attainable row singleton is measurable and the empirical row always lies in a finite support. This is a valid way to use finite-range independence, and it does not strengthen the hypothesis to raw-sample independence.

**Family-measurability scope:** the direct theorem explicitly requires the family map to be measurable and independent using `familyTestSpace d N δ`, the σ-algebra generated by the nonnegativity event and failure tests. Those measurable assumptions and the associated independence are mathematically sufficient for the result, but this file does not prove that a conventional σ-algebra on arbitrary random finite subsets of ℝ^S makes these particular family tests measurable or that a random family and row independent in that conventional model instantiate these binders automatically. Thus the direct theorem is a precise test-σ-algebra formulation, not yet a verified bridge from every usual random-finite-set formalization. The general code-based theorem similarly requires its stated section-measurability premises. This boundary is measurability/model compatibility, not an unproved numerical tail assumption.

For the principal theorem, Ω and μ specify an arbitrary probability space; State and Fintype are the finite source carrier; d is the normalized transition row; positive N and 0<δ<1 are source parameters; draw and its joint-law hypothesis give the source iid sampling; family is an a.e. nonnegative random finite set; `hfamily` is the explicit family measurability condition; and `hindep` is source row-level independence in the indicated σ-algebras. There is no hypothesis that the source probability bound or its pointwise deviation already holds. The final theorem’s `hV` is an almost-everywhere generalization of the source nonnegative-set assumption, subject to its measurability setup.
