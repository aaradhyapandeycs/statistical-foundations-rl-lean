## Independent reconstruction

For a fixed state-action transition row d and a nonnegative vector V, the source uses the **population** variance `v=E_d[V²]−(E_d[V])²`. Given N iid row samples, the centered empirical mean is `(P̂−P)V`. The source Bernstein lemma gives a strict simultaneous high-probability bound with radius `sqrt(2v·log(4|𝒱|/δ)/N) + 2‖V‖∞·log(4|𝒱|/δ)/(3N)` for a finite random family independent of the empirical row; its whole-matrix version additionally handles all SA rows. Separately, the later empirical Bernstein lemma uses the **empirical** variance and N−1 denominators. A fixed-vector population-variance tail alone does not establish either simultaneous statement.

## Findings

1. The imported `FiniteDistribution.variance` is exactly `E_d[V²]−(E_d[V])²`, and the proved centered-square identity justifies nonnegativity. `sampleValue_variance` identifies the measure-theoretic variance of each actual sampled value with this exact row quantity, not a variance proxy, an empirical variance, or an assumed estimate. The sample measure is the genuine iid finite product law from `EmpiricalRow`.
2. `sampleValue_independent` maps the actual independent coordinates through fixed V. `sampleSum_variance` derives N·v from independence, and `empiricalValue_variance` derives v/N for N>0 using the exact sample mean. The nonzero-denominator premise is explicit. `empiricalRow_chebyshev` then derives the actual row's two-sided polynomial tail without an assumed tail input. When v=0, `empiricalRow_zeroVariance_tail` proves every **positive** deviation event has probability zero, even if V differs on zero-mass states; no unjustified pointwise-everywhere assertion is made. A zero-norm vector is also covered by the separately proved pointwise-zero lemma in the imported Hoeffding module, but this staged module does not combine it into a single source-radius theorem.
3. The centered MGF factorization is for the actual iid sample law and equals the Nth power of the true centered row MGF. `empiricalRow_chernoff_mgf` proves the Chernoff step using this MGF, rather than assuming a tail bound. `centeredRow_mgf_le_exp_variance` proves `mgf(u)≤exp(u²v)` for `u≥0`, `uB≤1`, and `0≤V≤B`. The range restriction is explicit at that theorem; it follows from a proved bounded-exponential estimate, not a hidden hypothesis. The reflected vector B−V has the same true variance, so both tails and the two-sided union bound use the same v.
4. The final positive-B, positive-ε theorem chooses `u=ε/(2v+Bε)`, derives `u≥0` and `uB≤1` from v≥0, and proves the exact exponent `−N ε²(v+Bε)/(2v+Bε)²`. The denominator is positive because Bε>0, including v=0. `empiricalRow_variance_tail_norm` instantiates B with the finite supremum norm for nonnegative V and explicitly assumes positive norm. There is no extra MGF or tail premise in the resulting theorem.
5. The bound is genuinely weaker than a sharp source-constant Bernstein bound. For example at N=1, v=1/4, B=1 and ε=1/4, the proved exponent is −1/18, whereas the usual sharp Bernstein exponent `−Nε²/(2v+(2/3)Bε)` is −3/32. Thus the proved bound alone does not establish the printed radius, especially its leading variance constant and `2/3` linear coefficient. The file's opening note should be read as saying it does not establish the **source Bernstein theorem**: it does prove the weaker variance-sensitive exponential tail later in the file.
6. The final result remains for one fixed deterministic V and one row; it has no finite-family union, no independence of a random family from the empirical row, no δ/logarithmic source threshold or strict >1−δ event, and no simultaneous SA-row result. It also does not prove the later empirical-variance Bernstein bound. These are concrete missing results, not hypotheses silently carried by its tail theorem.

For `empiricalRow_variance_tail_explicit`, State and Fintype represent the finite row carrier, d carries the normalized nonnegative source probabilities, V is a deterministic state vector, N and `hN` specify positive iid sample count, B and `hB` give a positive range bound, ε and `hε` give a positive deviation threshold, and `hV` states the source-compatible vector range. The iid law is constructed in the imported sample measure; neither an MGF bound, an independent-family hypothesis nor a desired tail is supplied as a premise. The norm specialization obtains the source range from nonnegativity and the norm, with a visible positive-norm branch.
