## Findings

**Common seed and model choice.** `independentSeedLaw` is the product of the actual sample law and one fixed seed law. The averaging lemma sums over that same seed law, weights each fixed-seed testing guarantee by its atom mass, and only then uses finite averaging to choose one model whose total seeded risk is at least the testing lower bound. The seed law is supplied once and does not depend on the model index. The finite-kernel construction makes this operational for any fixed `κ : X → PMF Y`: its seed is one function `X → Y` with product coordinate laws, and `finiteKernelSeed_joint_law` identifies the entire sample/output joint law after evaluating the seed at the observed sample. The output PMFs may have arbitrary weights, including zero or irrational masses; a uniform finite seed is not being assumed.

**Large-family branch.** For each fixed seed, the existing finite-Fano theorem applies to the decoder induced by that fixed-seed estimator. The staged risk branch uses `Dᵢ ⊆ Rᵢ` from the pointwise decoder-error/Q-loss theorem, then invokes the averaging lemma. The conclusion is an actual product-law event for one hard model after averaging over seed. The Fano threshold is tied to exact family cardinality, with the large branch requiring `SA ≥ 16` and its stated `4096 N(1−γ)^3 ε² ≤ log K` budget.

**Small-family branch.** The two-point lemma averages the **sum** of the two oriented testing errors over the common seed. It does not attempt to average a fixed-seed maximum or choose the same winning model for every seed. Since the sum of the two averaged errors exceeds `1/5`, their maximum exceeds `1/10`; the branch then transfers whichever model's test error is larger to that model's decoder error and then to its actual policy-risk event. The test orientation is consistent: under the baseline law, guessing the active alternative is an error; under the alternative law, every non-alternative guess is an error. The source KL orientation is alternative relative to baseline and the strict lower bound uses the verified `KL ≤ 9/10` premise. The branch's `1024 N(1−γ)^3 ε² ≤ 9/10` budget is strict enough for `> 0.1`.

**Risk event and output scope.** The wrapper concludes for the actual full-sample/output joint law, with event `ε < ‖Q^π−Q*‖`, and the returned policy is `deterministicFamilyPolicy` of the selected function `Option (Fin m) → Fin (n+1)`. The pointwise lower-bound dependency first proves the strict gap in the opposite subtraction order and then uses norm symmetry; the event orientation therefore agrees. The finite-kernel wrapper uses the same event after mapping the product seed law to `(sample, κ(sample))`. Its model-independent data reconstruction is accounted for by the family observation bridge. The quantifiers cover every data-dependent PMF kernel over deterministic stationary policy outputs, including arbitrary sample-dependent weights. They do not cover policies that randomize actions at deployment, history-dependent policy outputs, or an estimator whose kernel is allowed to depend on the unknown model.

**Threshold split.** `correctedFamily_common_budget` uses the exact `SA=(m+1)(n+1)` and `K=1+mn` geometry. For `SA≥16`, `SA≤4K` implies the large-family Fano budget. For `SA<16`, `log(SA)<log16< 9/10·32` supplies the stronger small-family budget from the common `32768 N(1−γ)^3 ε² ≤ log(SA)` threshold. The branch assumptions and constants are consistent with the two risk theorem premises.

## Comparison with the printed source claim

The source theorem at `source/upstream/tex/chapters/generative_model_model_based.tex:1443–1464` assumes `SA≥16` and concludes probability at least `1/4` for a **deterministic policy estimator** (part (i)). Its proof restates the estimator as a deterministic policy estimate at lines 2085–2097. The unified finite-kernel wrapper has a weaker exported conclusion, strict probability `>1/10`, and includes the small-family branch `SA<16`; it therefore does not itself establish the printed theorem. In the large-family seeded branch, however, the proof obtains fixed-seed average decoder error at least `1/4` and the averaging lemma returns a model with seeded risk at least `1/4`; the current branch declaration only exposes the weaker `>1/10` consequence. This is the branch to use if matching the printed probability threshold on `SA≥16`.

The concrete action-randomizing counterexample in `docs/builder-generative-lower-bound-stochastic-policy-gap.md` checks out algebraically. With `m=n=1`, `γ=2/3`, `ε=1/100`, the staged probabilities are `p=5/6`, `q=379/450`. A stationary policy choosing the two actions with equal probability at the active state has stay probability `(p+q)/2=377/450` in either model, value `1/(1−γ(p+q)/2)=675/298`, while the alternative model's optimal value is `1/(1−γq)=675/296`. Its largest forced-first-action Q loss is attained at the alternative's good action and equals `γq(675/296−675/298)=379/44104<1/100`. Thus this same deployed randomized-action policy is ε-good in both the baseline and alternative models. The decoder-error-to-policy-risk implication used by the deterministic-output modules cannot be extended to that output class at the same parameters. This does not contradict the source theorem, whose policy output is specified as deterministic, and it does not refute a different construction for stochastic policies.
