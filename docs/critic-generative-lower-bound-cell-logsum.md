Source-level verdict: the first lemma is restricted to strictly positive inputs; the second validly extends the cell inequality to empty fibers. Neither is a Fano result.

`finite_positive_logSum` requires a nonempty finite index type and `a i>0`, `b i>0` at every index. Its normalization and weighted-log Jensen derivation prove the conventional oriented inequality `A log(A/B)≤Σ aᵢ log(aᵢ/bᵢ)`. It cannot be applied to a nonempty cell containing zero-mass atoms, and cannot by itself justify merging decoder outputs when input atoms may have zero mass.

`finite_cell_logSum` instead takes a `Finset` and positivity hypotheses only for members of that set. For a nonempty cell, the subtype of members is nonempty and inherits strict positivity, so the previous lemma applies. For an empty cell, both sums are zero and the Lean goal simplifies to equality. Thus it handles empty fibers/zero output cells while still requiring every atom inside a nonempty cell to have positive a and b mass. It is used by the finite-KL coarse-graining result whose input laws are globally full support; that theorem covers empty output fibers but does not cover general input laws with zero-mass atoms. Accordingly, this is enough for the stated full-support finite decoder route, not a zero-aware general KL data-processing theorem or an unconditional Fano proof.
