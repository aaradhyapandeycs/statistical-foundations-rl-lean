The source has N≥2, a finite nonnegative random vector family independent of the empirical row, success probability strictly greater than 1−δ, and radius `sqrt(2 log(4|𝒱|/δ)/(N−1)) sqrt(Var_P̂ V) + 7‖V‖ log(4|𝒱|/δ)/(3(N−1))`. The variance is that of the actual frequency row and therefore uses denominator N; the outside N−1 is not a redefinition of this variance.

This staged source proves the actual empirical frequency-row variance is nonnegative; its exact mean under the genuine iid sample law is `(N−1)/N` times the population variance, and for N≥2 the N/(N−1) correction is unbiased. It proves the deterministic B²/4 range bound and a deterministic comparison reducing empirical-variance error to first- and second-moment errors. These are genuine ingredients for studying empirical variance, not assumed statistical bounds.

`empiricalBernstein_fixedRow_smallConfidence` proves the exact source radius for a single nonnegative vector with probability one under the genuine iid law, including N=2, zero variance, and zero norm, in the explicitly restricted regime `3(N−1) ≤ 7 log(4/δ)` and δ>0. This follows because the linear part of the exact source radius is then at least the largest possible mean error. The source's full fixed-vector concentration inequality for arbitrary `0<δ<1` is not proved; in particular, the self-normalized exponential concentration linking population and empirical variances remains open. The random-family union/mixing extension and complete source lemma therefore also remain open. This bounded confidence case must not be represented as the general source result.
