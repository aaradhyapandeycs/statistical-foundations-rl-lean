It uses the independently established actual-trajectory/Bellman bridge, then the point-mass transition and action-independent reward at x; the equation `V(x)=(1−γ)u+γV(x)` and `γ<1` yield u. This covers γ=0 and does not require an optimal policy or positive policy mass on any action. It establishes the value at x, not equality with the original model at all states.

The empirical comparison assumes two complete sample tables agree for every action and sample index at all states other than x; data at x may differ freely. For a fixed common u, the two absorbing models have identical transition rows at x by absorption and away from x by equality of the corresponding actual empirical observations. The proof then uses identical rewards and discount and the proved uniqueness of the optimal Q Bellman fixed point, rather than assuming optimal Q equality. The fixed common u is essential: if u itself is selected differently from each data table, the hypothesis does not provide invariance.

The final equality concerns actual infinite-trajectory optimal values at every state. It compares each model's separately selected deterministic stationary greedy policy: the reviewed trajectory-optimality and policy-average-Q bridges identify its actual value with the maximum of that model's optimal Q row. Equal Q fixed points therefore give equal values regardless of tie-breaking. This supports the source's finite stationary policy framework; no pathwise equality of the two selected policies is claimed.

The module proves deterministic, pointwise invariance only. In particular, invariance with one common u does not justify using the sample-dependent choice `u=V̂*(x)` as an independent test vector.
