## Source domain and schedule

Model-free source Eq. `eq:thm:eta-TD` lines 180–185 specifies its bounds only for
1≤i≤T. Main TD theorem lines 205–218 explicitly assumes T≥2 and gamma∈[1/2,1).
The draft schedule has exactly these finite positive indices and exactly the
source lower denominator (c1 h T/log²T) and upper denominator (c2 h i/log²T).
It does not silently impose the lower schedule bound on the zero tail.

The unit-interval theorem uses only T≥2, gamma<1 and c1,c2>0, all entailed by
the source domain. It does not need c1≥c2, c1c2≤1/8, a sample-budget lower bound,
or an assumed desired cap. These are correctly not added. The standalone
unit-interval extension theorem transparently accepts finite [0,1] bounds;
the separate schedule theorem derives those bounds. At index zero the extension
preserves eta0 but the global unit-interval conclusion begins at index one,
matching the actual update's unused time-zero rate.

## Eligible terminal and late update indices

Source Lemma `lemma:TD-step-size` and subsequent Freedman steps use
T/(c2 logT)≤t, implicitly t≤T because rates are only specified through T. The
draft makes t≤T explicit, uses the exact real eligibility inequality, and defines
late natural indices by t/2+1≤k≤t. This is equivalent to real t/2<k for natural
indices, including odd t: floor(t/2)+1 is the first integer strictly after t/2.
It derives eta_k≤2log³T/(hT) directly from the upper schedule. The proof does not
assume the whole source step-size lemma or its disputed early-weight bound.
No feasibility conclusion t=T eligible is asserted: proving that requires
c2 logT≥1, which must come from a separately established budget or hypothesis.
When no t satisfies eligibility and t≤T, this helper's quantified implication
is merely vacuous; it cannot establish a final theorem at T without that step.

## Preserved actual objects

The extension is literally eta_i for i≤T and zero otherwise. The following
preservation statements cover all their indicated indices:

- Actual realized iterate V_t: update t uses rates 1,…,t, so equality for t≤T
  holds pointwise for every sample array, V0 and state. No sampling-law change.
- Scalar contraction at index i≤T: includes i=0 without needing eta0 bounded.
- Full rate weight at terminal t≤T and arbitrary k: k=0 initial weight retains
  its product over 1,…,t; 0<k≤t retains eta_k and product k+1,…,t; k>t is zero
  in both models without requesting eta_k agreement.
- Chronological error propagator with any start and t≤T: only actual updates
  start+1,…,t are read when start<t; start≥t is the same identity branch.
  Operator orientation and its input vector U are unchanged.
- Actual propagator rows: evaluation on the same coordinate basis vector
  transports the preceding equality, rather than replacing by abstract rows.
- Weighted row gamma eta_k times suffix row: retains k≤t≤T explicitly. At
  k>t this *unguarded* row definition can genuinely change because it still
  reads eta_k. The draft correctly does not claim arbitrary-k preservation for
  weighted rows, unlike the guarded scalar weight and guarded increment.

## Remaining consumption obligations

The file does not explicitly transport centered row noise, guarded fixed-terminal
weighted increments, padded increments, or the natural increment-history sigma
algebra. Their equality follows from the proven rate/iterate/propagator equalities
but still needs formal proof before rewriting a Freedman event proved for the
extension back to the original schedule. For centered noise at k≤t≤T the old
iterate is V_(k−1), also unchanged. At k=0 both noises are zero. For guarded
increments, k>t is zero in both even though an unguarded weighted row may differ.
All padded indices and coordinate maps must retain those guards.

Likewise a conditional-variance budget uses sigma algebras generated by the
increments. Equality of the guarded increment functions should imply equality
of their generated history, and conditional expectations can then be rewritten;
it is not enough to assert that the original *global* schedule has [0,1] rates.
The draft's zero-tail trick is valid, but exact stochastic-event transport and
through-T cumulative variance preservation are not exports of this file yet.
The actual product sample law itself depends only on M, policy and finite sample
horizon, not on eta, so the deterministic extension requires no probability-law
pushforward or new independence premise.

No strengthened source premise was found in the inspected statements. A final
consumer must discharge finite schedule and terminal feasibility rather than
retaining the extension's global helper premise in a source theorem.
