## Source reconstruction

For a fixed, possibly randomized policy, the algorithm samples N independent successors from each policy-induced kernel `Pπ(·|s)`, forms frequency rows `P̂π`, and outputs `(I−γ P̂π)⁻¹ rπ` using the known policy-averaged reward. It then claims a high-probability uniform error bound against the true policy value under the displayed sample-size condition and a sufficiently large universal constant. That latter claim has a precise `1−δ` probability, ε range, and `(1−γ)⁻³ ε⁻²` dependence; constructing an estimator alone does not prove it.

## Verdict and scope

The staged construction faithfully realizes a product-law version of the algorithm. `samplingLaw` is a finite product indexed by `(state, sample index)` of the policy-induced transition row measure; its marginal and mutual-independence theorems match that choice. `policyTransition` really is the policy-weighted mixture over action transitions. Product independence across states is a valid specific experiment and is stronger than merely specifying the within-state iid row marginals; the exports are scoped to this product law. The policy `π` is a fixed input to both law and estimator, so no data-dependent-policy evaluation is claimed. N>0 is explicit for frequency rows and the estimator; the product sample law itself can also be constructed at N=0.

The empirical row is the existing normalized count distribution, and the separately proved expectation is the arithmetic sample average. The empirical MDP assigns the same empirical policy row and known policy-averaged reward to every action. Its value fixed point under π consequently has precisely the source's empirical policy Bellman equation, and the uniqueness theorem applies to every competing vector solving that equation. This is a sound intrinsic characterization of the plug-in solution. The staged file does not prove the literal matrix-inverse formula or an invertibility identity; equality to that source notation relies on the standard discounted finite Bellman/inverse equivalence, which remains an explicit formal bridge if the literal expression is required.

The output is measurable in the finite sample data, with a fixed model and policy. It is also equal to the actual infinite-trajectory expected value in the empirical MDP via the previously established trajectory/fixed-point bridge; the theorem is not merely a finite-horizon surrogate. The model is an argument to the estimator, but its constructed transition field depends only on data, while reward and discount use known inputs. To check that this argument does not grant the estimator access to the unknown true kernel, an independent Lean probe proved equality of estimates for any two admissible MDPs with identical reward and discount, the same policy and data, regardless of their transition kernels. Joint measurability as those known inputs vary is not among the staged exports; data measurability for each fixed input is proved.

No result in this file bounds the random error against the actual true-model `trajectoryExpectedValue`, proves a concentration event, exhibits the source universal constant, or establishes the sharp sample-complexity theorem. In particular, the empirical-trajectory identity is not a statistical accuracy guarantee. The missing resolvent/perturbation, concentration, and variance analysis remain open exactly as recorded in the builder handoff. This review certifies the described construction and its scope, not the source theorem or an integrated export.
