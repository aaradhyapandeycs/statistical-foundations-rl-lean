## Exact source targets

For each `(s,a)`, lines 916–927 draw N iid samples from the actual transition row and define the empirical row using sample frequencies. For `0 < δ < 1`, finite `𝒱 ⊆ ℝ_+^S`, and `𝒱` independent of that empirical row:

- Hoeffding (lines 939–950) simultaneously bounds the absolute empirical-minus-true row evaluation for every `V ∈ 𝒱` by `‖V‖∞ sqrt(log(2|𝒱|/δ)/(2N))`, with probability **strictly greater** than `1−δ`.
- Bernstein (lines 956–966) uses `sqrt(2 log(4|𝒱|/δ)/N) sqrt(Var_{P_sa}(V)) + 2‖V‖∞ log(4|𝒱|/δ)/(3N)`, again simultaneously and with strict probability. Lines 969–976 add the all-rows vector inequality with log argument `4SA|𝒱|/δ`, assuming independence of `𝒱` and the **whole empirical matrix**.

Nonnegativity of V is material: sample values lie in `[0, ‖V‖∞]`, interval length `‖V‖∞`. Merely assuming `|V| ≤ ‖V‖∞` would double the interval length and fail to deliver the displayed Hoeffding constant. Fixed `𝒱` is trivially independent; the explicit independence clause also suggests random `𝒱`. The statement's log and `1/N` require particular care for `N=0` or `|𝒱|=0`; the text does not explicitly provide those positivity hypotheses. The empty-family assertion is vacuous, but a source-facing formalization must resolve its displayed undefined logarithm rather than silently restrict a frozen statement. For N=0, the empirical definition itself divides by N, so the usual sampling convention `N ≥ 1` needs explicit source interpretation.

## Verified Hoeffding route

`Mathlib/Probability/Moments/SubGaussian.lean:859–866` contains
`ProbabilityTheory.hasSubgaussianMGF_of_mem_Icc`: for `[IsProbabilityMeasure μ]`, a.e. measurable `X`, and a.e. `X ∈ [a,b]`, it gives the **centered** variable `X−∫X` sub-Gaussian MGF parameter `((‖b−a‖₊/2)^2)`. Boundedness supplies the required exponential integrability within that result.

`Mathlib/Probability/Moments/SubGaussian.lean:779–795` contains `ProbabilityTheory.HasSubgaussianMGF.measure_sum_ge_le_of_iIndepFun` and the `Finset.range` specialization `measure_sum_range_ge_le_of_iIndepFun`. The general form requires the centered family `iIndepFun X μ`, individual `HasSubgaussianMGF`, and a **nonnegative** threshold; it gives a one-sided `μ.real` bound `exp(−ε²/(2 Σ_i c_i))`, not directly a two-sided empirical average. With `X_i = V(sᶦ) − P_sa V`, each parameter is `B²/4`, `B=‖V‖∞`; taking the sum threshold `Nt` yields `exp(−2Nt²/B²)`. Apply the same theorem to negatives and union the tails to get the factor 2. Handle `B=0` separately: the vector and deviation are zero, avoiding division by zero and the degenerate sub-Gaussian parameter. Convert real-valued probabilities, ENNReal measures, and strict/non-strict events carefully.

`ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun.comp` (`Mathlib/Probability/Independence/Basic.lean:667–670`) preserves family independence under **measurable** coordinatewise maps; it can transport sample independence through fixed V evaluation and centering. `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_pi` (`Basic.lean:783–785`) provides coordinate independence on a finite product of probability measures, with each coordinate map a.e. measurable. These are applicable only after constructing the actual measure and finite random variables; the project's `FiniteDistribution` is a real probability vector, not by itself a `Measure` or `IsProbabilityMeasure` instance. Show its finite weights give a normalized measure and its expectation agrees with the finite sum, then prove the empirical frequency/average identity.

## Bernstein: no exact ready-made theorem found

A search of the pinned `Mathlib` source for `Bernstein`, `Bennett`, `subexponential`, `subgamma`, and variance-sensitive exponential tails found no probability theorem directly matching this bounded independent-sum Bernstein result. This is a bounded search conclusion, not proof that no equivalent declaration exists anywhere in the library. `ProbabilityTheory.variance_le_sq_of_bounded` (`Mathlib/Probability/Moments/Variance.lean:465`) gives a variance **upper bound**, not a variance-sensitive tail inequality. Chebyshev in that file is polynomial, so neither can replace the advertised exponential result or constants.

Relevant lower-level tools, verified by `#check`:

- `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun.mgf_sum` (`Mathlib/Probability/Moments/Basic.lean:365–378`) factors the MGF of a finite sum as a product. It requires family independence and **measurability of every variable**, even outside the particular finset in its signature. The related `iIndepFun.integrable_exp_mul_sum` at 346–362 requires a finite measure, measurability, and integrability for each summand exponential.
- `ProbabilityTheory.measure_ge_le_exp_mul_mgf` (`Basic.lean:410–430`) gives the one-sided Chernoff bound `μ.real{ε ≤ X} ≤ exp(−tε) mgf X μ t`, given a finite measure, `t ≥ 0`, and integrability of `exp(tX)`. The lower-tail variant is `measure_le_le_exp_mul_mgf` (`432–440`) for `t ≤ 0`.
- Existing project `Formalization/Probability/Variance.lean` defines `FiniteDistribution.variance`, proves it equals the expectation of the centered square and is nonnegative, and defines `DiscountedMDP.transitionVariance`. A missing bridge must identify this with the Mathlib variance of `V(sample)` under the constructed row measure, with actual integrability/finite support discharged.

A substantive missing theorem is a bounded, zero-mean variance-sensitive MGF estimate for each centered summand (or an equivalent Bernstein/Bennett theorem), including its domain, finite exponential integrability, the mgf product and Chernoff parameter optimization. For `[0,B]` values, `|X−EX| ≤ B`; with `σ²=Var_P(V)` the conventional two-sided tail `2 exp(−Nt²/[2σ²+2Bt/3])` leads to `t = sqrt(2σ² L/N) + 2BL/(3N)`. Each algebraic inequality and degenerate case (`B=0`, `σ²=0`, N positive) still requires proof. A sub-Gaussian theorem alone gives the wrong variance-sensitive scaling. No custom axiom or unproved concentration premise is a substitute.

## Union bounds, random families, and strict probability

`MeasureTheory.measure_biUnion_finset_le` and `measure_iUnion_fintype_le` (`Mathlib/MeasureTheory/OuterMeasure/Basic.lean:83–89`) give finite union bounds in ENNReal; they do not automatically prove event measurability, change from `μ.real`, or discharge the source logarithmic constants. For deterministic finite nonempty 𝒱, union two tails and k vectors; for all rows also union SA rows. No cross-row sample independence is needed for that last union once each row's own iid law is proved. The Bernstein log factor 4 leaves a factor-two failure-probability slack under the conventional two-tail bound (`≤ δ/2`), sufficient for the source's strict success probability when `δ>0`.

For a **random** finite family, a proof for each fixed V alone cannot be unioned as if the random members were deterministic. Construct a measurable random finite set (including measurability of membership, cardinality, deviation-event and nonnegative vector coordinates, e.g. via a suitable finite-set representation/enumeration) and use its independence from the exact empirical row or entire empirical matrix named in the source. A full matrix-independent family is independent of each empirical row by measurable projection. The source asks for independence of the family and **empirical row**, not the stronger assumption that it is independent of the ordered raw sample sequence; preserving this scope may use the finite-support distribution of the empirical row and a product-law/finite-conditioning argument. Countably many cardinality cases may be needed if a random finite family's size is not fixed; silently imposing an almost-sure fixed cardinality would narrow the statement. Joint law and measurability details remain unproved.

For Hoeffding, the displayed factor 2 gives the usual elementary union bound `failure ≤ δ`, which only directly yields success `≥ 1−δ`, not the source's **strict** `> 1−δ`. The finite state/N sampling model has finite empirical-row support; for each fixed V with positive B the strict failure event `|deviation| > threshold` has a positive finite-support gap above the threshold, permitting application of the non-strict tail bound at a strictly larger threshold and thus a strict estimate. The B=0 case is trivial. This is a proposed route; carry the strict argument through finite union and, for random 𝒱, measurable conditioning/integration without introducing extra assumptions. Alternatively provide another valid strict-tail argument. Do not claim the plain `≤ δ` union bound proves strict success.
