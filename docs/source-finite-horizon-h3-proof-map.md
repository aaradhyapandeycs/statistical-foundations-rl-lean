# Source map: sharp finite-horizon H³ policy-learning proof

## Sources and theorem match

Primary comparison: Li, Wei, Chi, and Chen, [*Breaking the Sample Size Barrier in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning with a Generative Model*, arXiv:2005.12900v8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.12900v8), finite-horizon Section 7 and Appendix C.1. The pinned monograph is `source/upstream/tex/chapters/generative_model_model_based.tex` (SHA-256 `b7e7abfb2c2cb2c0dd82470db5ecac3a67d7163682246d499935d9130214ac4d`); its finite-horizon extension is lines 2501–2585 and explicitly refers readers to Li et al. for the full proof.

The statements match at the main level. Li et al. Theorem 4 (PDF p. 10, equations (19)–(20)) and the monograph `Thm:sample-compl-finite` both use the exact empirical transition model and empirical optimal planning, independent generative samples for every `(s,a,h)`, known normalized rewards, nonstationary finite horizon, `0<ε≤H`, and simultaneous all-state/all-stage V and Q loss at most ε with per-tuple sample order `H³ log(HSA/δ)/ε²`. The monograph states `δ>0`; the paper's sample bound has the same logarithmic form. The total number of calls is `HSA N`, hence the `H⁴` total-sample order.

Form `P̂_h`; plan by backward dynamic programming. The fitted decision at stage h depends only on stages h through H and is independent of rows at earlier stages. |

| Auxiliary variance sequence | §7.2, p. 20, equations (61)–(62) | For each fixed policy π, set `V_{H+1}^{(l)}=V̂_{H+1}^{(l)}=0`; level 0 is the ordinary true/empirical policy value with reward `r_h^π`. ## Tie-breaking and independence requirements

The independence step for π̂* is causal, not a blanket claim that the fitted policy is independent of all data. At a fixed stage h, only its suffix decisions `π̂*_j`, `j≥h+1`, enter `V_{h+1}^{(l)}`. Backward DP makes those decisions functions of rows at stages `j≥h+1`; independent generative calls make this suffix independent of the current stage-h row. The concentration event must hold uniformly over `(s,a)` because the chosen action `π̂*_h(s)` can depend on the stage-h data. A deterministic local argmax tie rule (or an independently randomized rule that preserves the same suffix measurability) supports this factorization. A globally selected optimizer whose tie-breaking uses earlier-stage samples could break it. The paper states the backward-DP independence property, but does not give a formal measurable tie-breaking construction; that is an implementation detail a formal proof must make explicit.

The true optimal policy π* is fixed by the true MDP and can be chosen independently of data. The algorithm's π̂* is the exact empirical optimizer; approximate planning would add an optimization-gap term and is outside this statement. Stage rewards are known and shared by true/empirical models, and the samples from different rows/stages are independent; within-row i.i.d. alone would not justify the future/current-row independence argument.

## Gaps for the current Lean frontier

The staged residual-accumulation/optimal-transfer modules prove deterministic perturbation bounds under a common residual envelope. They do not formalize the auxiliary variance sequence, Lemma 9's Bernstein condition, the Lemma 12 growth bound, or the C.1 recursive unrolling that removes one horizon factor. Thus they support the transfer from a probability event to policy loss but are not a formalization of this H³ proof. A coarse uniform row-residual envelope of order `H/√N` passed through an additional H accumulation gives H⁴; the sharp H³ proof needs the variance recursion and its data-dependent-policy independence argument.

The published paper's C.1 proof depends on Lemma 12, whose proof is Appendix C.2. Section 7 plus C.1 alone is therefore not a complete source for the sharp rate; a source-facing formalization must also pin and audit Lemma 12/C.2. It must separately show the selected policy's stagewise suffix measurability, uniform rowwise Bernstein events for all levels and both policies, the two-event confidence allocation, and the final source constants/logarithm conversion. The monograph itself cites the external proof rather than reproducing these steps; its displayed theorem's all-stage V/Q quantifiers and sample domain remain the target.
