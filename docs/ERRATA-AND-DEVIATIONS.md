# Errata and deviations

This record compares the formalized statements and proof steps with Chen, Chi, Fan, Li, Wei, and Yan, *Statistical Foundations of Reinforcement Learning: A Non-Asymptotic Perspective*, arXiv:2610.41903v1. Source locations refer to that pinned version, using its LaTeX labels and chapter files. The accompanying manuscript is the [pinned arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41903v1). Last updated 2026-11-10.

Every erratum derives from a candidate in [SOURCE-ISSUES.md](SOURCE-ISSUES.md); the deviations draw on the same ledger, including its statement-index scope disclosures, and on the statement index. The candidates were found and checked by independent AI-agent review against the pinned TeX. None has been confirmed by the authors, and no entry here is an author-approved erratum. An entry is listed among the errata when a printed statement, a printed intermediate assertion in a proof, or a printed formula is contradicted in some case, and is then classed A to D by how it is contradicted (see Errata below); only class A entries are printed statements shown false in a non-degenerate case under their natural reading. Where a named Lean refutation exists, the entry names it and its file; otherwise the entry says that the counterexample is a paper argument. An ill-posed printed claim is listed only when an implicit assertion it depends on is shown false (Erratum 22, class D). A missing argument for a true conclusion, a chosen reading of ambiguous text, a completion of an undefined algorithm step, or a restriction of a proved variant is a deviation. Counts are numbered record entries, not the number of repeated occurrences; related occurrences are grouped. Editorial typography is not counted. The source ledger retains further candidates, such as proof gaps with true conclusions, that are not counted here.

## arXiv:2610.41903v1

### Errata

Entries are grouped by class, which says how strongly the printed text is contradicted. Entry numbers are unchanged from earlier versions of this record (each entry carries its class), so the numbering within a class is not consecutive. Class A: a printed statement is false in a non-degenerate case under its natural reading. Class B: it fails only at a boundary or degenerate case, or outside a parameter range assumed elsewhere in the book. Class C: a proof step is false while the stated conclusion survives. Class D: the printed form is ambiguous, ill-posed or undefined at the point, which is a question for the authors rather than a refutation. A Lean refutation under a stated reading (class D) or at a boundary (class B) is not a claim that the printed statement is false in the intended setting.

#### Class A: printed statement false in a non-degenerate case under its natural reading

10. **Chapter 5, Lemma 5.22, `lemma:sqv`, online-RL lines 3612–3615.** *(candidate; AI-agent review; not author-confirmed)* **Class A.** Printed statement false in a non-degenerate case under its natural reading. The second variance bound uses coefficient 1 for a general signed random variable. For `X=1` with probability `1/8` and `X=−1` with probability `7/8`, `|X|=1`, `Var(X)=7/16`, and `Var((X−E[X])²)=63/64`, so the printed inequality fails. The Lean counterexample is `centeredSquare_measure_coefficient_one_false` in [CenteredSquareVarianceMeasure.lean](../Formalization/Counterexamples/CenteredSquareVarianceMeasure.lean); coefficient 4 is proved in [SquaredVarianceBounds.lean](../Formalization/Probability/SquaredVarianceBounds.lean). See [C14](SOURCE-ISSUES.md#C14). **Effect:** the second clause is replaced by the valid general coefficient-4 bound; the first clause is unchanged.

23. **Chapter 7, statistical inference, confidence sets, `sec:inference` and `eq:convex.Cdelta`, `inference.tex` lines 150–213.** *(candidate; AI-agent review; not author-confirmed)* **Class A.** Printed statement false in a non-degenerate case under its natural reading. (the data-selected-set, ellipsoid-threshold and singular plug-in items are class D) The rule "choose any convex set with conditional mass at least 1−α under N(V̄_T, Λ̂_T/T)" does not give approximate confidence regions when the set is chosen from the same data: for an exactly Gaussian estimator, sign-selected half-lines with mass 1−α have coverage 1−2α for every T. The printed rectangles apply the √T scaling twice (half-width R̂/√T), so their coverage tends to 0; the printed ellipsoid threshold R² (R the χ²(d) quantile) is over-conservative for conventional α and too small only when R<1, and `d` is undefined; the plug-in Λ̂_T is undefined when some state is unsampled, an event of positive probability. For the oracle-covariance variant this is machine-checked in the book's own one-state TD model: the sign-selected half-lines are convex, read only the data, have mass exactly 1−α under N(V̄_T, Λ/T), and eventually cover with probability at most Φ(z) − (1 − Φ(z))/2 < 1−α (`oneState_signSel_coverage_eventually_lt` in [HalfLineEvidence.lean](../Formalization/TDInference/Confidence/HalfLineEvidence.lean)); the version with the printed plug-in Λ̂_T remains a paper argument. The rectangle under-coverage is machine-checked on one instance (the same one-state model, S = 1): with R̂ at most the (1−α)-quantile of \|X\|, X ~ N(0, Λ/T) or, pathwise, N(0, Λ̂_T/T), the printed rectangle's coverage tends to 0 for every 0 < α < 1 (`c47_rectangle_printed_undercovers`, `c47_rectangle_printed_undercovers_plugin` in [PrintedRectangleEvidence.lean](../Formalization/TDInference/Confidence/PrintedRectangleEvidence.lean)); the finite-T figures and the S-dimensional ℓ∞ quantile are not formalized. See [C47](SOURCE-ISSUES.md#C47). **Effect:** none of the printed confidence-set constructions is formalized. Proved instead (`Eq_convex_Cdelta_corrected_oracle`, partial): with the oracle covariance Λ = A⁻¹Γ_trueA⁻ᵀ (unknown in practice) and a shape fixed before the data, one- and two-sided intervals for a linear functional uᵀV^π and a Bonferroni box, with coverage error O(T^(-1/3)). With the same oracle covariance, corrected fixed convex shapes, ellipsoids (threshold q, or the χ²(S) quantile, not R²) and rectangles (half-width r/√T), including exactly calibrated 1−α levels, are proved with coverage error K₀·T^(-1/3) (instance constant; `Eq_convex_Cdelta_*_corrected_oracle*`, strengthened 2026-11-10 from an extra log(2T) factor). Consistency of the plug-in Λ̂_T for the corrected covariance A⁻¹Γ_trueA⁻ᵀ is machine-checked, qualitatively and without a rate (`Eq_defn_hat_Lambda_corrected`; the printed Λ⋆ target fails under [C43](SOURCE-ISSUES.md#C43)), and the singular event of (4) is shown to have positive but vanishing probability (`pathProb_aBar_singular_pos`, `pathProb_aBar_singular_le`). With the printed plug-in Λ̂_T, fixed-shape intervals and the Bonferroni box are proved asymptotically valid (`Eq_convex_Cdelta_corrected_plugin`; finite-T rate T^(-1/3) with an instance constant, also for the plug-in ellipsoid and ℓ∞ rectangle, the rectangle constant depending on α; Γ_true ≻ 0 assumed; Lean's junk Λ̂_T = 0 on the singular event lies inside the charged bad event). A valid rectangle rule (the printed one fails, above), the printed ellipsoid, a finite-T rate for the printed plug-in rule with arbitrary C_α, and a finite-T definition of the procedure on the singular event remain open. Theorem 7.11 itself is not affected by this entry ([C43](SOURCE-ISSUES.md#C43) covers it).

### Proof route and scope

Several source proofs receive supplementary arguments without changing their conclusions. These include a deterministic low-sample case for the omitted intermediate range in the policy-evaluation proof, and a stricter testing estimate that supplies the strict inequality used in the two-state testing reduction. These proof supplements are not counted as errata because the final claims remain valid under the reviewed source conditions. The ledger also records false intermediate steps in proofs whose conclusions survive, for example in the Theorem 4.7 lower-bound proof ([C31](SOURCE-ISSUES.md#C31)) and the Theorem 5.19 tail bound ([C26](SOURCE-ISSUES.md#C26)); they are paper findings without Lean refutations and are not counted here. Theorem 3.9 (`Thm:sample-compl-finite`, model-based chapter lines 2551–2570) is no longer listed as a deviation: `Thm3_9` in [Ch3.lean](../Formalization/Statements/Ch3.lean) states it as printed, for every deterministic policy that is optimal for the empirical MDP at every stage and state, with no tie rule fixed and no `N>0` premise. The earlier positive-`N` and causal-tie-rule entries `Thm3_9_positiveN` and `Thm3_9_causal_positiveN` are retained. The implementation also proves algorithm-specific bounds through explicit finite probability models and actual trajectory or sample laws rather than treating concentration estimates as assumptions.
