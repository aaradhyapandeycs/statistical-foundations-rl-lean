## Source reconstruction

For one fixed state-action pair `(s,a)`, let d=P(·|s,a) be a normalized law on the finite state space. The source draws N independent identically distributed states from d and defines the empirical mass at s′ as N⁻¹ times the number of samples equal to s′. Its frequency formula requires N>0; no nonzero lower bound on individual probabilities is needed. For a fixed real state vector V, evaluating the empirical row equals N⁻¹∑ᵢV(Xᵢ), and its expectation equals the original row's evaluation. The source next discusses concentration simultaneously over finite sets and, separately, all rows; those further statements impose their own independence and probability conditions and are not consequences of unbiasedness alone.

## Findings

1. `rowMeasure` (25–31) turns the original finite-distribution mass into a probability measure via the previously defined PMF with mass `ENNReal.ofReal (d.prob s)`; nonnegativity and sum one are fields of `FiniteDistribution`, not additional hypotheses. `sampleMeasure` (33–40) is the actual N-fold product measure, not a family of merely equal marginal measures. `sampleMeasure_coordinate` and `_singleton` (42–54) show each coordinate has the correct row mass, and `sampleMeasure_independent` (56–59) proves mutual independence of the N coordinate random variables. Together these are the source's iid sample law for one fixed row.
2. `empiricalRow` (61–71) uses precisely `(N : ℝ)⁻¹ * ∑ i, if ω i = s then 1 else 0`, with a proof of `0<N`, and proves nonnegative normalized finite masses. There is no zero-denominator default. The product law itself also exists for N=0, but the source frequency row cannot be formed at N=0; this distinction is correct.
3. `empiricalRow_expectation` (73–80) proves the row evaluation equals the sample mean pointwise for every observation and every real V. `rowMeasure_integral`, `sampleMeasure_coordinate_integral`, and `empiricalRow_unbiased` (82–105) identify the actual product-measure expectations and prove unbiasedness for every fixed deterministic V. V is not restricted to nonnegative entries, so the result includes the source's nonnegative vectors. The proof makes no positivity assumption about d(s′).
4. The definition is parameterized by an arbitrary finite law `d`, so one may instantiate it with a specific MDP transition row. A single jointly sampled full `SA × S` transition matrix is not defined here, and no independence across rows is claimed. More importantly, the Hoeffding and Bernstein inequalities in source lines 939–974, random finite-vector-set independence, their constants, simultaneous quantifiers, and high-probability conclusions are not proved by this file. In particular unbiasedness for deterministic V does not establish the source's data-independent random-set concentration bounds. These remain explicit coverage obligations.

For `empiricalRow_unbiased`, the implicit carrier and Fintype instance represent the source finite state space; `d` is the fixed source transition row (including probability nonnegativity and normalization); `N` is the source sample count; `hN` makes explicit the source formula's positive denominator; and `V` is an arbitrary fixed state vector. There is no hidden concentration conclusion, supplied iid premise, witness, or proof-step assumption: the iid law is constructed as the product measure. The per-coordinate theorems instead take `i : Fin N` and `s : State`, expressing the source quantification without an endpoint shift.
